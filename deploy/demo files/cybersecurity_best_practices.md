# Cybersecurity Awareness & Best Practices Guide

A practical guide to understanding common cyber threats and the everyday habits that reduce risk for individuals and organizations.

## Why Cybersecurity Awareness Matters

Most successful cyberattacks don't rely on advanced hacking — they rely on human error. A single clicked link, reused password, or unpatched device can open the door to data theft, financial loss, or system-wide compromise. Awareness is the first and cheapest line of defense.

## Core Principles

- **Confidentiality** – Information is accessible only to those authorized to see it.
- **Integrity** – Data remains accurate and unaltered by unauthorized parties.
- **Availability** – Systems and data remain accessible to authorized users when needed.

These three principles form the **CIA Triad**, the foundation of most security frameworks.

## Common Threats at a Glance

| Threat | What It Does |
|---|---|
| Phishing | Tricks users into revealing credentials via fake messages or sites |
| Ransomware | Encrypts files and demands payment to restore access |
| Malware | Damages or exploits systems through malicious software |
| Man-in-the-Middle | Intercepts communication between two parties |
| SQL Injection | Manipulates databases through malicious input |
| DDoS | Floods a system with traffic to make it unavailable |

## Personal Best Practices

1. **Use strong, unique passwords** for every account — consider a password manager.
2. **Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA)** wherever it's offered.
3. **Keep software and operating systems updated** to patch known vulnerabilities.
4. **Think before you click** — verify senders before opening links or attachments.
5. **Avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive tasks**, or use a trusted VPN.
6. **Back up important data regularly**, ideally with an offline or air-gapped copy.
7. **Lock devices** when unattended and use full-disk encryption.
8. **Review app and browser permissions** periodically.

## Organizational Best Practices

- Apply the **principle of least privilege** — give users only the access they need.
- Conduct **regular security awareness training** for all staff.
- Maintain an **incident response plan** and test it periodically.
- Use **network segmentation** to limit the spread of a breach.
- Deploy **firewalls, IDS/IPS, and endpoint protection** across the network.
- Perform **regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing**.
- Keep an **asset inventory** so nothing goes unpatched or unmonitored.

## Incident Response Checklist

- [ ] Identify and contain the affected system(s)
- [ ] Preserve logs and evidence for investigation
- [ ] Notify relevant stakeholders and, if required, regulators
- [ ] Eradicate the root cause (malware, compromised credentials, etc.)
- [ ] Restore systems from clean backups
- [ ] Conduct a post-incident review and update defenses

## Quick Reference: Spotting Phishing

- Mismatched or suspicious sender addresses
- Urgent or threatening language pressuring quick action
- Generic greetings instead of your actual name
- Links that don't match the claimed destination when hovered
- Unexpected attachments, especially `.exe`, `.zip`, or macro-enabled files

## Further Notes

Cybersecurity is not a one-time setup but an ongoing practice. Threats evolve constantly, so awareness, patching, and monitoring need to be continuous rather than periodic.
